What does a distributed LID system deliver to the receiving water—and when?

That question motivates HydroCouple's new development: **LID as a storage-node attribute** in OpenSWMM and SWMMVis.

We are moving from evaluating individual practices toward their combined effects across the drainage network. A new set of animated examples explores two chained LIDs, receiving-water backwater, controlled detention, a second storm and valve failure.

The tradeoffs matter: longer holding can increase calculated treatment, but it can also shift or raise the release peak. And pollutant left in storage is different from pollutant treated. Every drain, bypass and loss belongs in the assessment.

The article includes GIF animations, six reproducible models and a SWMMVis tutorial. The examples use hypothetical treatment kinetics, with water and pollutant balance checks.

It closes with the next connection: the new spatially explicit groundwater model, distributed recharge and surface-water–groundwater feedbacks. We will explore those receiving-water implications in future articles.
